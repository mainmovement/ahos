

# Document 04

# AHOS Workflow Catalog (AWC)

## Master Workflow Registry

### Version 1.0

---

# 1. Purpose

This document defines the official workflow architecture of AHOS.

Every workflow implemented inside the platform shall be registered in this catalog before development begins.

The catalog provides a centralized registry that defines the responsibility, ownership, inputs, outputs, dependencies, execution rules, and lifecycle of every workflow.

No workflow may exist outside this catalog.

---

# 2. Workflow Philosophy

AHOS is intentionally designed as a collection of many small, independent workflows rather than a few large monolithic automations.

Each workflow should solve one specific problem.

Each workflow should remain independently testable, replaceable, maintainable, and deployable.

System intelligence emerges through collaboration between workflows rather than through complexity inside a single workflow.

---

# 3. Workflow Engineering Principles

Every workflow shall follow these engineering rules.

### Single Responsibility

One workflow.

One mission.

One primary output.

---

### Loose Coupling

A workflow should not depend on the internal implementation of another workflow.

Only defined interfaces may be used.

---

### High Cohesion

All operations inside a workflow must contribute to the same business objective.

---

### Independent Deployment

Every workflow should be deployable individually.

---

### Independent Testing

Every workflow must support isolated testing.

---

### Independent Recovery

Failure of one workflow shall never stop the entire platform.

---

### Versioning

Every workflow shall maintain its own version number.

---

### Traceability

Every execution shall be logged.

---

### Replaceability

Any workflow may be replaced without redesigning the architecture.

---

# 8. Workflow Communication Rules

Workflows communicate only through approved mechanisms.

Allowed communication methods:

Database

Webhook

Queue

Internal Events

API

Direct workflow-to-workflow dependencies should be minimized.

---

# 9. Error Handling Policy

Every workflow must support:

Retry

Graceful Failure

Logging

Recovery

Notification

No workflow should silently fail.

---

# 10. Performance Principles

Each workflow should:

Consume minimal RAM.

Consume minimal CPU.

Minimize API requests.

Minimize AI token usage.

Avoid unnecessary database writes.

Support long-term execution.

The platform is optimized for continuous execution on a single personal laptop.

---

# 11. Human Approval Policy

Workflows may analyze.

Workflows may recommend.

Workflows may learn.

Workflows may generate improvement proposals.

However,

No workflow may modify production configurations, prompts, scoring models, architecture, or other workflows without explicit operator approval received through Telegram.

---

---

# 13. Workflow Evolution

AHOS is expected to grow.

New workflows may be added whenever intelligence capabilities expand.

However,

Existing workflow responsibilities should remain stable.

Prefer adding a new workflow over making an existing workflow excessively complex.

---

# 14. Architectural Law

The intelligence of AHOS shall emerge from the cooperation of many small, specialized workflows.

No workflow should become a monolithic automation.

The platform shall always prioritize:

* Simplicity
* Modularity
* Maintainability
* Scalability
* Reliability
* Observability
* Independent evolution

over unnecessary complexity.
