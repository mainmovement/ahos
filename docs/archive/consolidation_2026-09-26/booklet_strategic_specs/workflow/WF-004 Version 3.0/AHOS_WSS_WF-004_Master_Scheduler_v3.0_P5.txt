AHOS Workflow Specification Sheet
WF-004 — Health Monitor
Version 1.0
Phase 5 — Composite Health Evaluation & Dependency Awareness
1. Purpose
1.1 Objective

This phase establishes the architectural framework governing composite health evaluation, dependency-aware health calculation, hierarchical health propagation, and overall platform readiness assessment.

The Health Monitor SHALL derive system-wide health from authoritative component assessments while preserving deterministic evaluation and dependency integrity.

1.2 Scope

This phase governs:

Composite Health
Dependency Health
Health Propagation
Runtime Readiness
Service Dependency Evaluation
Critical Component Assessment
Overall Platform Health
Dependency Integrity

Corrective actions remain outside the scope of this specification.

2. Composite Health Philosophy

Platform health SHALL be an architectural conclusion derived from component health rather than an independently measured value.

The platform SHALL NOT be considered healthy if mandatory architectural dependencies are unhealthy.

3. Composite Evaluation Principles

Composite health evaluation SHALL remain:

deterministic;
hierarchical;
dependency-aware;
reproducible;
auditable;
technology-independent.

Component health SHALL remain the only authoritative input for composite evaluation.

5. Dependency Awareness

Health evaluation SHALL explicitly recognize dependency relationships.

A component SHALL NOT be classified as Fully Healthy if one or more mandatory dependencies remain unavailable.

Dependency relationships SHALL remain explicitly defined.

6. Critical Dependencies

The Health Monitor SHALL distinguish between:

Mandatory Dependencies

Failure directly impacts operational capability.

Optional Dependencies

Failure reduces functionality but SHALL NOT invalidate overall operational readiness.

Dependency classification SHALL remain policy-driven.

7. Health Propagation

Health degradation MAY propagate upward through the architectural hierarchy.

Propagation SHALL preserve:

dependency relationships;
severity;
assessment evidence;
architectural traceability.

Health propagation SHALL NEVER propagate downward.

8. Runtime Readiness

The Health Monitor SHALL continuously determine whether the Runtime Engine is operationally ready.

Runtime readiness SHALL require successful evaluation of all mandatory runtime components.

Readiness SHALL remain distinct from component availability.

9. Composite Evaluation Rules

Composite evaluation SHALL:

evaluate each component independently;
validate dependency integrity;
aggregate authoritative health states;
derive overall platform health;
preserve assessment determinism.

Evaluation order SHALL NOT influence the final result.

10. Partial Failure Handling

Partial component failures SHALL remain isolated whenever possible.

Failure of a non-critical component SHALL NOT automatically invalidate unrelated architectural domains.

Failure isolation SHALL preserve operational continuity.

11. Health Stability

Temporary fluctuations SHALL NOT immediately produce unstable platform health transitions.

The Health Monitor MAY require confirmation before publishing significant health state changes.

Confirmation policies SHALL remain implementation-specific.

12. Composite Assessment Audit

Every composite evaluation SHALL generate immutable audit evidence including:

Composite Assessment Identifier
Platform Health State
Component Summary
Dependency Evaluation Result
Assessment Timestamp
Correlation Identifier

Composite assessments SHALL remain permanently reconstructable.

13. Composite Invariants

The following SHALL remain continuously true.

Dependency Invariant

Mandatory dependencies SHALL participate in every composite evaluation.

Hierarchy Invariant

Composite health SHALL always be derived from lower-level authoritative assessments.

Propagation Invariant

Health propagation SHALL preserve dependency direction.

Readiness Invariant

Operational readiness SHALL depend upon mandatory architectural components only.

Determinism Invariant

Equivalent health inputs SHALL always produce identical composite health results.

15. Phase 5 Completion Statement

Phase 5 establishes the architectural model governing composite health evaluation across the AHOS platform.

It defines how individual component health assessments are combined, validated, and propagated to produce a deterministic, dependency-aware representation of overall platform health.

By separating component assessment from platform-level health evaluation, this phase enables scalable operational monitoring while preserving architectural clarity, dependency integrity, and long-term extensibility.
