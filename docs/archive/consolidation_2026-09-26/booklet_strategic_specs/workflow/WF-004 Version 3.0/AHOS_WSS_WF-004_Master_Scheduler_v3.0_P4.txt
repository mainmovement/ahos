AHOS Workflow Specification Sheet
WF-004 — Health Monitor
Version 1.0
Phase 4 — Health Assessment Lifecycle & Monitoring Coordination
1. Purpose
1.1 Objective

This phase establishes the architectural lifecycle governing health assessment scheduling, monitoring coordination, reassessment behavior, evaluation frequency, and operational continuity.

Health assessment SHALL remain continuous throughout the operational lifetime of the AHOS platform.

1.2 Scope

This phase governs:

Health Assessment Lifecycle
Assessment Scheduling
Monitoring Frequency
Event-Driven Reassessment
Continuous Monitoring
Assessment Coordination
Health Freshness
Monitoring Continuity

Alert processing remains outside the scope of this phase.

2. Monitoring Philosophy

Health SHALL represent a continuously maintained operational fact.

Health SHALL NEVER be considered permanently valid.

Every health assessment SHALL eventually expire and require reassessment.

3. Monitoring Principles

Health monitoring SHALL remain:

continuous;
deterministic;
lightweight;
event-aware;
time-aware;
interruption-resistant.

Monitoring SHALL minimize operational overhead while preserving assessment accuracy.

4. Health Assessment Lifecycle

Every health assessment SHALL progress through the following lifecycle.

Assessment Scheduled
        │
        ▼
Evidence Collection
        │
        ▼
Evidence Validation
        │
        ▼
Rule Evaluation
        │
        ▼
Health Classification
        │
        ▼
Publication
        │
        ▼
Health Expiration
        │
        ▼
Reassessment Scheduled

Every assessment SHALL complete this lifecycle before replacement by a subsequent assessment.

5. Assessment Scheduling

The Health Monitor SHALL support multiple assessment triggers including:

periodic assessment;
event-driven assessment;
startup assessment;
recovery assessment;
manual assessment.

Scheduling strategy SHALL remain implementation-independent.

6. Periodic Assessment

Periodic assessments SHALL maintain continuous operational visibility.

Assessment frequency SHALL be configurable.

Configuration SHALL NOT alter assessment semantics.

7. Event-Driven Assessment

Health reassessment SHALL occur whenever significant architectural events occur.

Examples include:

workflow registration changes;
execution failures;
scheduler interruptions;
database connectivity changes;
infrastructure degradation;
dependency recovery.

The list above SHALL NOT be considered exhaustive.

8. Assessment Freshness

Every health assessment SHALL possess a validity period.

Expired assessments SHALL NOT be considered authoritative.

Freshness SHALL be evaluated independently for each monitored component.

9. Continuous Monitoring

Health monitoring SHALL continue throughout the operational lifetime of the platform.

Temporary monitoring interruptions SHALL generate monitoring continuity records.

Monitoring continuity SHALL be recoverable.

10. Monitoring Coordination

Concurrent health assessments MAY execute simultaneously.

Assessment coordination SHALL guarantee:

component isolation;
deterministic results;
duplicate prevention;
consistent publication.

Concurrent assessments SHALL NOT interfere with one another.

11. Reassessment Policy

Health reassessment SHALL occur when:

assessment validity expires;
monitored evidence changes;
architectural events require reevaluation;
manual reassessment is authorized.

Reassessment SHALL replace, but SHALL NOT overwrite, historical assessments.

12. Assessment Failure

Failure of an individual assessment SHALL NOT terminate the overall monitoring process.

Failed assessments SHALL be recorded for diagnostic purposes.

Monitoring SHALL remain resilient.

13. Monitoring Continuity

The Health Monitor SHALL maintain continuous monitoring history including:

assessment start;
assessment completion;
assessment interruption;
reassessment scheduling;
publication events.

Continuity SHALL remain auditable.

14. Lifecycle Audit

Every lifecycle transition SHALL generate immutable audit evidence including:

Assessment Identifier
Lifecycle Stage
Timestamp
Component Identifier
Correlation Identifier
Assessment Outcome

Lifecycle history SHALL remain permanently reconstructable.

15. Monitoring Invariants

The following SHALL remain continuously true.

Lifecycle Invariant

Every assessment SHALL complete the defined lifecycle.

Freshness Invariant

Authoritative health information SHALL always remain current.

Continuity Invariant

Monitoring SHALL remain continuous throughout platform operation.

Isolation Invariant

Concurrent assessments SHALL remain independent.

History Invariant

Historical assessments SHALL NEVER be overwritten.

17. Phase 4 Completion Statement

Phase 4 establishes the lifecycle architecture governing health assessment within the AHOS platform.

It defines how health assessments are scheduled, executed, refreshed, coordinated, and audited to ensure that health information remains continuously valid, operationally relevant, and architecturally consistent.

By introducing a formal health assessment lifecycle, this phase ensures that monitoring evolves from isolated health checks into a persistent, deterministic, and enterprise-grade operational process.
