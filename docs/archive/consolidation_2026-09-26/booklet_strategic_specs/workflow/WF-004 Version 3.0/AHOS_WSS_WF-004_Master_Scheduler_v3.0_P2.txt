AHOS Workflow Specification Sheet
WF-004 — Health Monitor
Version 1.0
Phase 2 — Health State Model & Assessment Framework
1. Purpose
1.1 Objective

This phase establishes the architectural model governing health state definition, health assessment, health classification, and operational readiness evaluation across the AHOS platform.

Health SHALL represent the architectural ability of a component to perform its assigned responsibilities.

1.2 Scope

This phase governs:

Health State Model
Health Classification
Health Evaluation
Operational Readiness
Component Assessment
Composite Health
Health Consistency
Health State Transition

Corrective actions remain outside the scope of this phase.

2. Health Philosophy

Health SHALL represent operational capability rather than mere availability.

A component SHALL be considered healthy only if it can successfully fulfill its architectural responsibilities.

Availability alone SHALL NOT imply health.

3. Health Principles

Health assessment SHALL remain:

deterministic;
continuous;
evidence-based;
technology-independent;
auditable;
reproducible.

Subjective health evaluation SHALL NOT occur.

4. Health State Model

Every monitored component SHALL exist in exactly one architectural health state.

The authoritative health states SHALL be:

Unknown
    │
    ▼
Healthy
    │
 ┌──┴──────────┐
 ▼             ▼
Degraded    Maintenance
 │             │
 └──────┬──────┘
        ▼
Unhealthy
        │
        ▼
Unavailable

No additional health states SHALL exist without architectural revision.

5. Unknown State

Unknown SHALL indicate insufficient evidence to determine component health.

Unknown SHALL NOT be interpreted as Healthy.

The Health Monitor SHALL continue assessment until sufficient evidence becomes available.

6. Healthy State

A component SHALL be classified as Healthy only when:

all mandatory operational checks succeed;
architectural responsibilities are fulfilled;
required dependencies are operational;
no critical policy violations exist.
7. Degraded State

A component SHALL be classified as Degraded when:

primary functionality remains operational;
one or more non-critical conditions reduce operational quality;
architectural responsibilities can still be fulfilled.

Degraded components SHALL remain operational but SHALL require increased observation.

8. Maintenance State

Maintenance SHALL indicate an intentionally restricted operational condition.

Maintenance SHALL be considered an authorized state rather than a failure condition.

9. Unhealthy State

A component SHALL be classified as Unhealthy when:

one or more critical architectural requirements fail;
operational integrity cannot be guaranteed;
execution reliability becomes uncertain.

Unhealthy components SHALL NOT automatically be considered unavailable.

10. Unavailable State

Unavailable SHALL indicate that the component cannot provide its architectural responsibilities.

Unavailable SHALL represent complete operational unavailability.

11. Composite Health

System Health SHALL be derived from individual component health assessments.

Composite health SHALL consider:

Runtime Layer Health
Database Health
Infrastructure Health
External Dependency Health
Resource Health

Composite health SHALL be deterministic.

12. Health State Transition Rules

Health state transitions SHALL occur only after successful completion of health evaluation.

Transitions SHALL be explicit.

Direct transitions that bypass the evaluation process SHALL NOT occur.

13. Operational Readiness

The Health Monitor SHALL continuously evaluate whether each component is operationally ready to perform its assigned architectural responsibilities.

Operational readiness SHALL be assessed independently from component startup status.

14. Health Assessment Integrity

Every health assessment SHALL preserve:

assessment timestamp;
component identity;
assessment evidence;
resulting health state;
correlation identifier.

Health conclusions SHALL be traceable to supporting evidence.

15. Health Consistency

Equivalent assessment inputs SHALL always produce identical health classifications.

Health evaluation SHALL remain deterministic across repeated assessments.

16. Health Invariants

The following SHALL remain continuously true.

State Invariant

Every monitored component SHALL have exactly one current health state.

Assessment Invariant

Every health state SHALL be supported by verifiable assessment evidence.

Transition Invariant

Every health transition SHALL follow the defined architectural model.

Consistency Invariant

Health evaluation SHALL remain deterministic.

Composite Invariant

Composite system health SHALL always be derived from authoritative component health states.

18. Phase 2 Completion Statement

Phase 2 establishes the architectural health state model for the AHOS platform.

It defines how component health is classified, evaluated, transitioned, and aggregated into a deterministic system-wide health assessment while preserving architectural consistency and operational transparency.

This model provides the canonical health framework upon which all subsequent monitoring, alerting, metrics, and operational governance capabilities SHALL be built.
