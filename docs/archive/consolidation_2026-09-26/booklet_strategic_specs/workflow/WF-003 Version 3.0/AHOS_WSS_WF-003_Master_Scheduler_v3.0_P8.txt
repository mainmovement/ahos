AHOS Workflow Specification Sheet
WF-003 — Execution Manager
Version 1.0
Phase 8 — Observability, Metrics & Operational Excellence
1. Purpose
1.1 Objective

This phase establishes the architectural observability model governing execution visibility, operational metrics, telemetry, auditability, and runtime diagnostics within the AHOS Runtime Engine.

The Execution Manager SHALL continuously expose sufficient operational evidence to enable monitoring, troubleshooting, capacity planning, and architectural analysis.

1.2 Scope

This phase governs:

Execution Metrics
Operational Telemetry
Runtime Statistics
Diagnostic Information
Structured Logging
Execution KPIs
Operational Visibility
Observability Readiness

Operational dashboards remain implementation-specific.

2. Observability Philosophy

Execution SHALL always be observable.

Lack of visibility SHALL be treated as an architectural defect.

Operational evidence SHALL be generated automatically.

Human operators SHALL NEVER reconstruct execution history manually.

3. Observability Principles

Execution observability SHALL satisfy:

completeness;
determinism;
consistency;
traceability;
auditability;
low operational overhead.

Observability SHALL remain independent from business logic.

4. Execution Metrics

The Execution Manager SHALL expose metrics including, but not limited to:

Active Executions
Completed Executions
Failed Executions
Cancelled Executions
Timed-out Executions
Average Execution Duration
Queue Waiting Time
Dispatch Latency
Recovery Count
Retry Count

Additional metrics MAY be introduced without affecting execution semantics.

5. Operational Telemetry

Operational telemetry SHALL include:

lifecycle transitions;
dispatch events;
recovery events;
capacity events;
governance events;
persistence events.

Telemetry SHALL be chronological and immutable.

6. Structured Logging

Every operational log SHALL include, at minimum:

Timestamp
Execution Identifier
Workflow Identifier
Event Type
Severity
Lifecycle State
Correlation Identifier
Runtime Owner

Logs SHALL be machine-readable.

7. Diagnostic Context

Every diagnostic event SHALL preserve sufficient context to enable deterministic root cause analysis.

Diagnostic context SHALL include:

execution identity;
workflow version;
execution phase;
current lifecycle state;
related architectural component.
8. Execution KPIs

The Execution Manager SHALL calculate operational KPIs including:

Execution Success Rate
Average Dispatch Time
Average Recovery Time
Execution Failure Ratio
Retry Effectiveness
Capacity Utilization
Queue Efficiency
Runtime Availability

KPI calculation SHALL remain deterministic.

9. Observability Interfaces

The Execution Manager SHALL expose operational information through implementation-independent interfaces.

The architectural model SHALL remain compatible with:

monitoring platforms;
metrics collectors;
audit systems;
reporting services.

Interface technology SHALL remain implementation-specific.

10. Historical Analytics

Historical execution information SHALL remain available for:

trend analysis;
capacity planning;
performance optimization;
incident investigation;
architectural improvement.

Historical evidence SHALL remain immutable.

11. Alert Readiness

The Execution Manager SHALL expose sufficient operational information to enable future alert generation.

Alert rules SHALL remain external to the Execution Manager.

WF-003 SHALL expose facts, not decisions.

12. Operational Health Indicators

The Execution Manager SHALL continuously maintain indicators describing:

execution throughput;
execution backlog;
runtime responsiveness;
recovery effectiveness;
governance activity;
persistence health.

Health interpretation SHALL remain the responsibility of WF-004 Health Monitor.

13. Performance Baseline

The Execution Manager SHALL maintain operational baselines enabling future comparison of:

execution latency;
dispatch latency;
recovery duration;
completion throughput;
capacity utilization.

Baseline storage SHALL support longitudinal analysis.

14. Observability Audit

Every observability record SHALL preserve:

Observation Identifier
Observation Timestamp
Execution Identifier
Observation Category
Metric Reference
Correlation Identifier

Observability evidence SHALL remain reconstructable.

15. Observability Invariants

The following SHALL remain continuously true.

Visibility Invariant

Every execution SHALL remain observable throughout its lifecycle.

Metric Invariant

Operational metrics SHALL accurately reflect committed execution state.

Logging Invariant

Structured logs SHALL remain chronologically ordered.

Audit Invariant

Observability evidence SHALL remain immutable.

Independence Invariant

Observability SHALL NEVER modify execution behavior.

16. Phase 8 Compliance Matrix
Requirement	Mandatory
Structured Logging	SHALL
Operational Metrics	SHALL
Telemetry	SHALL
KPI Calculation	SHALL
Historical Analytics	SHALL
Diagnostic Context	SHALL
Immutable Audit	SHALL
Technology Independence	SHALL

Failure of any mandatory requirement SHALL invalidate Phase 8 compliance.

17. Phase 8 Completion Statement

Phase 8 establishes the observability architecture of the AHOS Execution Manager.

It defines how execution information is measured, recorded, exposed, and preserved to support monitoring, diagnostics, operational excellence, and future optimization while maintaining strict separation between execution behavior and operational analysis.

By providing deterministic metrics, structured telemetry, and immutable operational evidence, this phase ensures that the Execution Manager remains transparent, measurable, and maintainable throughout the lifecycle of the AHOS platform.
