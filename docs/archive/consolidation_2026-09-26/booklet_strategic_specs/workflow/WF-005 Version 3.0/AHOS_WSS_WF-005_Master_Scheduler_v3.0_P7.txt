AHOS Workflow Specification Sheet
WF-005 — Data Acquisition Manager
Version 1.0
Phase 7 — Acquisition Orchestration, Flow Control & Resource Coordination
1. Purpose
1.1 Objective

This phase establishes the architectural framework governing acquisition orchestration, flow control, workload coordination, and resource management for external data acquisition.

The Data Acquisition Manager SHALL coordinate acquisition activities in a controlled, scalable, and deterministic manner.

1.2 Scope

This phase governs:

Acquisition Orchestration
Flow Control
Workload Coordination
Queue Coordination
Resource Allocation
Backpressure Management
Concurrency Coordination
Throughput Stability

Provider-specific execution remains outside the scope of this specification.

2. Orchestration Philosophy

Acquisition SHALL be coordinated as a managed operational process rather than as isolated API requests.

Resource consumption SHALL remain predictable under varying operational loads.

3. Orchestration Principles

Acquisition orchestration SHALL remain:

deterministic;
scalable;
fair;
observable;
auditable;
policy-driven.

Operational load SHALL NEVER compromise architectural integrity.

4. Acquisition Queue

Every acquisition request SHALL enter an architectural acquisition queue before execution.

The queue SHALL preserve:

request ordering;
acquisition priority;
correlation identifiers;
scheduling metadata.

Queue implementation SHALL remain technology-independent.

5. Concurrency Management

The Data Acquisition Manager SHALL coordinate concurrent acquisitions according to architectural policy.

Concurrency SHALL consider:

provider capacity;
resource availability;
operational priorities;
rate-limit constraints.

Concurrency SHALL remain configurable.

6. Flow Control

Flow control SHALL regulate acquisition throughput to prevent:

provider overload;
resource exhaustion;
uncontrolled request bursts;
acquisition instability.

Flow control SHALL remain deterministic.

7. Backpressure Management

The architecture SHALL support controlled backpressure whenever acquisition demand exceeds processing capacity.

Backpressure policies MAY include:

request postponement;
queue buffering;
priority scheduling;
controlled throttling.

Backpressure SHALL preserve platform stability.

8. Workload Prioritization

Acquisition workloads SHALL support explicit priority classes.

Minimum priority levels SHALL include:

Critical
High
Normal
Low

Priority policies SHALL remain configurable.

9. Resource Coordination

The Data Acquisition Manager SHALL coordinate the utilization of:

network resources;
provider connections;
acquisition workers;
execution capacity.

Resource contention SHALL remain controlled.

10. Operational Stability

Acquisition orchestration SHALL continuously preserve:

throughput stability;
predictable latency;
workload fairness;
operational consistency.

Temporary workload spikes SHALL NOT destabilize the acquisition architecture.

11. Observability

The orchestration layer SHALL expose operational metrics including:

Queue Depth
Active Acquisitions
Waiting Requests
Throughput
Resource Utilization
Concurrency Level
Backpressure Status

Metrics SHALL remain implementation-independent.

12. Orchestration Audit

Every orchestration decision SHALL preserve immutable audit evidence including:

Queue Identifier
Scheduling Timestamp
Priority Class
Resource Assignment
Orchestration Decision
Correlation Identifier

Audit history SHALL remain permanently reconstructable.

13. Orchestration Invariants

The following SHALL remain continuously true.

Queue Invariant

Every acquisition SHALL pass through the architectural queue.

Concurrency Invariant

Concurrent acquisition SHALL remain policy-controlled.

Flow Control Invariant

Throughput SHALL remain regulated.

Backpressure Invariant

Resource exhaustion SHALL trigger controlled backpressure rather than uncontrolled degradation.

Audit Invariant

Every orchestration decision SHALL remain fully traceable.

Failure of any mandatory requirement SHALL invalidate Phase 7 compliance.

15. Phase 7 Completion Statement

Phase 7 establishes the orchestration architecture of the AHOS Data Acquisition Manager.

It defines how acquisition requests are coordinated, prioritized, scheduled, and executed while preserving deterministic behavior, resource efficiency, operational stability, and complete traceability.

By introducing queue-based orchestration and flow control, this phase enables the AHOS platform to scale from a small number of providers to a large and dynamic ecosystem without architectural redesign.
