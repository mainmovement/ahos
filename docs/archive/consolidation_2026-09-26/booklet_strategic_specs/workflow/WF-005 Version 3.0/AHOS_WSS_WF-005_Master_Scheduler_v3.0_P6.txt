AHOS Workflow Specification Sheet
WF-005 — Data Acquisition Manager
Version 1.0
Phase 6 — Data Identity, Acquisition Traceability & Lineage Foundation
1. Purpose
1.1 Objective

This phase establishes the architectural framework governing data identity, acquisition traceability, and lineage preservation for all externally acquired information entering the AHOS platform.

Every accepted acquisition SHALL receive a unique and immutable architectural identity before downstream processing.

1.2 Scope

This phase governs:

Acquisition Identity
Data Traceability
Lineage Foundation
Acquisition Provenance
Correlation Management
Identity Preservation
Data Origin Tracking
End-to-End Traceability

Transformation lineage remains outside the scope of this phase.

2. Identity Philosophy

Every accepted acquisition SHALL become a uniquely identifiable architectural object.

Identity SHALL remain stable regardless of downstream processing.

3. Identity Principles

Acquisition identity SHALL remain:

globally unique;
immutable;
deterministic;
auditable;
provider-independent;
technology-independent.

Identity SHALL NEVER be regenerated after acceptance.

4. Acquisition Identity

Every accepted acquisition SHALL receive an immutable Acquisition Identifier.

The identifier SHALL uniquely represent the accepted acquisition event within the AHOS platform.

Duplicate identifiers SHALL NOT exist.

5. Correlation Identity

Every acquisition SHALL preserve a Correlation Identifier linking all related operational events, including:

acquisition request;
provider response;
quality assessment;
downstream processing;
audit records.

Correlation SHALL remain continuous throughout the processing lifecycle.

6. Source Provenance

The following provenance information SHALL be permanently preserved:

Original Provider
Endpoint Identifier
Provider Version
Source Timestamp
Acquisition Timestamp
Request Identifier
Response Identifier (if available)

Provenance SHALL NEVER be modified after acceptance.

7. Lineage Foundation

WF-005 SHALL establish the first lineage record for every accepted acquisition.

This record SHALL become the root of the complete AHOS data lineage.

Subsequent workflows SHALL extend the lineage but SHALL NOT replace its origin.

8. Identity Preservation

Downstream workflows SHALL preserve the original Acquisition Identifier.

Additional processing identifiers MAY be introduced, but the original identity SHALL remain continuously available.

9. Traceability

Every accepted acquisition SHALL remain traceable from:

Original Provider
Original Request
Runtime Events
Quality Evaluation
Normalization
Feature Engineering
Intelligence
Simulation
Decision
Learning

Traceability SHALL support complete reconstruction of processing history.

10. Audit Foundation

Identity information SHALL support:

incident investigation;
regulatory audit;
historical replay;
dataset reconstruction;
explainability.

Audit capability SHALL remain implementation-independent.

11. Identity Constraints

WF-005 SHALL NOT:

regenerate acquisition identity;
remove provenance;
replace correlation identifiers;
modify lineage origin.

Identity integrity SHALL remain absolute.

12. Identity Invariants

The following SHALL remain continuously true.

Identity Invariant

Every accepted acquisition SHALL possess exactly one immutable Acquisition Identifier.

Provenance Invariant

Every acquisition SHALL preserve complete source provenance.

Correlation Invariant

Operational correlation SHALL remain continuous across all processing stages.

Lineage Invariant

Every accepted acquisition SHALL represent the root of an immutable lineage chain.

Traceability Invariant

Every acquisition SHALL remain reconstructable from origin to final processing outcome.

13. Phase 6 Compliance Matrix
Requirement	Mandatory

Lineage Foundation	SHALL
End-to-End Traceability	SHALL
Identity Integrity	SHALL
Audit Readiness	SHALL

Failure of any mandatory requirement SHALL invalidate Phase 6 compliance.

14. Phase 6 Completion Statement

Phase 6 establishes the identity and traceability architecture of the AHOS Data Acquisition Manager.

It defines how externally acquired information receives a permanent architectural identity and becomes the root of an immutable lineage chain that supports end-to-end traceability across the entire AHOS platform.

By introducing acquisition identity and provenance at the earliest possible stage, this phase enables deterministic auditing, historical replay, explainability, and trustworthy downstream processing without requiring later reconstruction of data origins.
