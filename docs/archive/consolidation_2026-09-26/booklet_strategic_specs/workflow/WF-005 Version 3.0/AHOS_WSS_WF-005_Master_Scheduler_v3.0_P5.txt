AHOS Workflow Specification Sheet
WF-005 — Data Acquisition Manager
Version 1.0
Phase 5 — Acquisition Resilience, Fault Tolerance & Recovery Coordination
1. Purpose
1.1 Objective

This phase establishes the architectural framework governing acquisition resilience, fault tolerance, recovery coordination, and operational continuity during external provider failures.

The Data Acquisition Manager SHALL maintain acquisition continuity while preserving architectural consistency and complete traceability.

1.2 Scope

This phase governs:

Fault Detection
Failure Classification
Retry Coordination
Recovery Readiness
Provider Isolation
Operational Continuity
Fault Evidence Preservation
Resilience Policies

Business decision-making remains outside the scope of this specification.

2. Resilience Philosophy

External provider failures SHALL be considered normal operational conditions rather than exceptional events.

The architecture SHALL tolerate temporary failures without compromising platform integrity.

3. Resilience Principles

Acquisition resilience SHALL remain:

deterministic;
observable;
fault-tolerant;
recoverable;
auditable;
implementation-independent.

Recovery SHALL preserve architectural correctness rather than maximize acquisition speed.

4. Failure Classification

The Data Acquisition Manager SHALL classify acquisition failures.

Minimum categories SHALL include:

Communication Failure
Authentication Failure
Timeout
Rate Limit
Invalid Response
Partial Response
Provider Unavailable
Unknown Failure

Additional categories MAY be introduced through future revisions.

5. Retry Coordination

Retry behavior SHALL be governed by architectural policy.

Retry coordination SHALL consider:

Failure Category
Provider Status
Retry Eligibility
Retry Interval
Maximum Retry Attempts

Retry execution SHALL remain deterministic.

6. Provider Isolation

Failure of one provider SHALL NOT interrupt acquisition from independent providers.

Fault isolation SHALL preserve:

acquisition continuity;
provider independence;
operational stability.
7. Recovery Coordination

Following a recoverable failure, the Data Acquisition Manager MAY initiate recovery according to architectural policy.

Recovery SHALL preserve:

Acquisition Identifier
Correlation Identifier
Source Attribution
Failure History

Recovery SHALL NEVER erase historical failure evidence.

8. Failure Evidence

Every acquisition failure SHALL preserve immutable evidence including:

Failure Identifier
Provider Identifier
Failure Category
Failure Timestamp
Acquisition Identifier
Correlation Identifier

Failure evidence SHALL remain permanently available for audit and analytics.

9. Operational Continuity

Temporary acquisition failures SHALL NOT compromise the operational integrity of the AHOS platform.

Downstream workflows SHALL receive consistent operational information regarding acquisition outcomes.

10. Escalation Readiness

The architecture SHALL support escalation of repeated or critical acquisition failures.

Escalation policies SHALL remain external to WF-005.

WF-005 SHALL expose sufficient operational evidence for future notification and governance workflows.

11. Recovery Audit

Every recovery attempt SHALL generate immutable audit information including:

Recovery Identifier
Related Failure Identifier
Recovery Timestamp
Recovery Outcome
Retry Count
Correlation Identifier

Audit history SHALL remain reconstructable.

12. Recovery Invariants

The following SHALL remain continuously true.

Failure Invariant

Every acquisition failure SHALL be classified.

Retry Invariant

Every retry SHALL follow architectural policy.

Isolation Invariant

Provider failures SHALL remain isolated whenever possible.

Evidence Invariant

Failure history SHALL NEVER be discarded.

Continuity Invariant

Operational continuity SHALL remain preserved despite temporary provider failures.

14. Phase 5 Completion Statement

Phase 5 establishes the resilience architecture of the AHOS Data Acquisition Manager.

It defines how acquisition failures are detected, classified, isolated, and coordinated for recovery while preserving deterministic behavior, operational continuity, and complete historical traceability.

By treating provider failures as expected operational events rather than exceptional conditions, this phase enables AHOS to maintain reliable data acquisition in dynamic and imperfect external environments.
