AHOS Workflow Specification Sheet
WF-005 — Data Acquisition Manager
Version 1.0
Phase 3 — Multi-Source Acquisition, Prioritization & Coordination
1. Purpose
1.1 Objective

This phase establishes the architectural framework governing coordinated acquisition from multiple external providers, source prioritization, redundancy management, and acquisition consistency.

The Data Acquisition Manager SHALL coordinate multiple providers while preserving deterministic behavior and source traceability.

1.2 Scope

This phase governs:

Multi-Source Acquisition
Source Prioritization
Redundant Providers
Source Coordination
Acquisition Scheduling
Provider Selection
Acquisition Consistency
Cross-Source Independence

Data validation remains outside the scope of this phase.

2. Multi-Source Philosophy

No external provider SHALL be considered permanently authoritative.

The architecture SHALL support acquisition from multiple providers simultaneously.

Provider replacement SHALL NOT require architectural redesign.

3. Coordination Principles

Multi-source acquisition SHALL remain:

deterministic;
scalable;
provider-independent;
fault-tolerant;
auditable;
reproducible.

Provider implementation details SHALL NOT influence architectural behavior.

4. Source Priority Model

Every registered provider SHALL possess an explicit priority level.

Priority MAY consider:

reliability;
response latency;
operational availability;
supported capabilities;
architectural policy.

Priority SHALL remain configurable.

5. Provider Selection

Before acquisition begins, the Data Acquisition Manager SHALL determine the most appropriate provider based upon:

provider availability;
supported capability;
architectural priority;
acquisition policy.

Selection SHALL be deterministic.

6. Redundant Providers

Multiple providers MAY supply equivalent information.

Redundant acquisition SHALL support:

failover readiness;
data continuity;
provider resilience;
operational flexibility.

Redundancy SHALL NOT introduce duplicate acquisition records.

7. Parallel Acquisition

The architecture SHALL support concurrent acquisition from multiple providers.

Parallel acquisition SHALL preserve:

acquisition isolation;
correlation identifiers;
independent failure handling;
deterministic completion.

Concurrent acquisitions SHALL NOT interfere with one another.

8. Acquisition Coordination

The Data Acquisition Manager SHALL coordinate provider usage while respecting:

provider priorities;
rate limits;
acquisition windows;
operational policies;
resource constraints.

Coordination SHALL prevent unnecessary provider contention.

9. Provider Failover

If the preferred provider becomes unavailable, the Data Acquisition Manager MAY select an alternative registered provider according to architectural policy.

Failover SHALL preserve:

acquisition traceability;
provider attribution;
correlation identifiers.

Provider failover SHALL NEVER conceal the original acquisition path.

10. Acquisition Consistency

Equivalent acquisition requests SHALL produce consistent coordination decisions under equivalent operational conditions.

Acquisition consistency SHALL remain deterministic.

11. Cross-Provider Independence

The failure of one provider SHALL NOT interrupt acquisitions performed by unrelated providers.

Provider isolation SHALL preserve platform availability.

12. Source Attribution

Every acquisition SHALL permanently preserve:

Selected Provider
Provider Priority
Provider Version
Acquisition Route
Failover Status
Acquisition Timestamp

Source attribution SHALL NEVER be removed.

13. Coordination Invariants

The following SHALL remain continuously true.

Priority Invariant

Every acquisition SHALL follow an explicitly defined provider priority.

Attribution Invariant

Every acquisition SHALL preserve complete provider attribution.

Isolation Invariant

Provider failures SHALL remain isolated whenever possible.

Redundancy Invariant

Redundant providers SHALL NOT create duplicate accepted acquisitions.

Determinism Invariant

Equivalent acquisition conditions SHALL always produce identical provider selection outcomes.

15. Phase 3 Completion Statement

Phase 3 establishes the multi-source acquisition architecture of the AHOS platform.

It defines how multiple external providers are coordinated, prioritized, and utilized while preserving deterministic acquisition behavior, provider independence, operational resilience, and complete source traceability.

By introducing structured provider coordination and failover readiness, this phase enables the Data Acquisition Manager to support future expansion toward diverse market data ecosystems without requiring architectural redesign.
